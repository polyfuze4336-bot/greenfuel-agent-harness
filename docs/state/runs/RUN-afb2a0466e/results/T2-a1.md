## Residency

**F1 [HC-01 / SEC-001], High: not assessable.**
- FACT: SEC-001 states it cannot be accepted until D-009 exists. The proposal says the same.
- The legal meaning of "jurisdiction" (storage, access, metadata, telemetry, AI inference) is an open human decision. The cell boundary is therefore provisional.
- Any acceptance claim before D-009 would be a rejection trigger under HC-01.

**F2 [HC-01 / SEC-001], High: key custody is undefined.**
- The proposal says key management is "placed and administered inside" the jurisdiction. R5 admits that key custody design is open.
- The unresolved points are who holds the root of trust, whether keys or key material can leave the cell, and whether the cloud management plane is operated from outside.
- Key management is a flow that SEC-001 requires on the inventory.
- The proposal references F-01 to F-15 only generically and does not map its own flows to them. I could not check the matrix.
- Cheapest test: key-management and management-plane flows listed per port with configuration evidence, plus a negative test from outside the jurisdiction.

**F3 [HC-01 / SEC-002], High: support and break-glass access is an assumption.**
- The assumption "in-jurisdiction support and break-glass staffing exists" (D-033) is not a control.
- There is no stated deny-by-default mechanism.
- There is no stated binding between a break-glass use and an approval, or separation of the approver from the user (SEC-002 needs a log and an approval record).
- Cheapest test: an outside access attempt is denied, and a break-glass use produces both a log and an approval record.

**F4 [HC-01 / SEC-001], Medium: the anchoring design conflicts with itself.**
- Anchors must be "readable by a verifier without the application" and "independent". They must also stay in-jurisdiction.
- Trusted timestamping and transparency logs are usually external services. The proposal itself notes that hashes of raw data may count as in-scope (ASSUMPTION).
- Any external anchor is a cross-border flow needing a D-011 entry, or must be dropped.

**F5 [HC-01], Medium: backups, disaster recovery, logs and telemetry have no mechanism.**
- R6 and the table assert in-jurisdiction placement but give no design. The proposal relies on "restore tests", which do not exist yet.
- Logs (SEC-014) are not placed in the trace table.
- Edge-to-shore relays (F-11) are required to be in-jurisdiction, with no mechanism stated.
- ASSUMPTION: the proposal does not say whether disaster recovery means a second site in the same jurisdiction exists. That is unknown (D-010).

An Azure region choice is not residency evidence, and the proposal correctly does not rely on one.

## Tenant isolation

**F6 [HC-02 / SEC-006], High: operator-owned common evidence is unresolved.**
- D-015 and D-016 are open. Evidence common to a delivery involves the operator and shipowners. If a shared record or index is chosen, it violates HC-02 ("any index, cache… shared").
- Do not accept Option B until the isolation model for that evidence is decided.

**F7 [HC-02], High: cell-level shared components are unaddressed.**
- Per-shipowner containers, keys, index and compute are proposed. These shared elements are not mentioned:
  - the identity service and key-management service per cell;
  - cell-level log and telemetry views;
  - the independent verifier, which reads every shipowner's chains;
  - batched anchors that span shipowners;
  - edge relays and brokers (F-11) if they are multi-tenant.
- Each could be a shared log view or compute context.
- Cheapest test: enumerate every cell-level component and mark it per-shipowner or shared.

**F8 [SEC-006], Medium: no adversarial test is planned.**
- SEC-006 requires adversarial tests with zero cross-tenant disclosures on each release and on a schedule.
- The proposal's trace table does not mention SEC-006, so no test is planned. Isolation "by structure" is a sound approach but is unproven.
- No agent or prompt-injection path is described. This area is not assessable because the proposal does not define any agent or AI inference over the store.

## Tamper evidence

**F9 [HC-08 / SEC-008], High: the append-only guarantee rests on "role design".**
- "Enforced at the storage layer and by role design" is only a convention if a privileged administrator can bypass it.
- The real detective control is the external anchor, which is open (D-013).
- Between anchors there is a window where an administrator could rewrite the tail undetected.
- Cheapest test: a privileged administrator modifies or deletes a record, and the verifier must detect it.

**F10 [HC-08 / SEC-008], Medium: chain coverage is per delivery.**
- A hash chain inside a delivery does not by itself show that a whole delivery, or the chain tail, was deleted.
- A per-cell or per-shipowner sequence anchored independently would close that gap. This is not specified.
- Reordering and replay are listed in verification scope, but no mechanism is described.

**F11 [HC-08 / FR-001 / FR-006], Medium: the first record is the weak point.**
- Hashes show integrity, not authenticity. Signing depends on D-019, and meters may not sign. Manual entry may exist.
- R4 admits edge compromise and clock error corrupt the first record, and the mitigation is unverified.
- FR-006 needs the shore copy to match edge hashes and sequence gaps reported. The mechanism is stated but untested.

**F12 [SEC-008], Medium: retention and deletion conflict with append-only.**
- Retention and legal hold are unknown (D-015). Expiry deletion must be a logged, detectable operation and cannot be silent.

**F13, Low: verifier independence.**
- It is unspecified who operates the verifier and from where. An outside verifier would breach SEC-002 and SEC-001.

## Controls that are only conventions or assumptions
- Immutability by role design.
- In-jurisdiction staffing for support and break-glass.
- "Gaps reported, never filled silently".
- Mitigations R4 to R6, which are marked unverified.
